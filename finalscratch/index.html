1. First: create your Scratch account

Scratch is a free platform for creating interactive stories, games, and animations.

You need an account so you can save your work online and eventually share your final project.

  1. Open Join Scratch .
  2. Create a username. Do not use your real name.
  3. Create a password you can remember and keep private.
  4. Complete the requested account information.
  5. Enter the requested email address and complete email confirmation.
  6. Sign in to Scratch.

Scratch's registration instructions tell users not to use their real name as a username and to keep passwords private.

STOP & DO: Do not continue until you can sign in to your Scratch account.

4. Your first program

Programs are instructions. Scratch lets you build those instructions by snapping blocks together.

when green flag clicked say “Welcome to my data lab!” for 2 seconds move 50 steps say “We have a problem to solve.” for 2 seconds
  1. Click your sprite.
  2. From Events, drag out when green flag clicked.
  3. From Looks, attach a say ___ for 2 seconds block.
  4. Change the words.
  5. From Motion, attach move 10 steps and change 10 to 50.
  6. Add another say block.
  7. Click the Green Flag.
STOP & DO: Build and run the program before continuing. Change the dialogue so it sounds like you.

5. What just happened?

EVENT

When green flag clicked tells Scratch when to begin.

SEQUENCE

Scratch runs connected instructions in order— from top to bottom.

RESULT

Your sprite talks and moves because your instructions caused those actions.

Programming idea #1: EVENT → INSTRUCTIONS → RESULT

6. Predict before you run

What will happen if you move the move 50 steps block so it comes before the first say block?

TRY IT: Rearrange the blocks and run the code. Then put them in whatever order you prefer.

7. Make it yours

Scratch calls programmable characters and objects sprites.

You can choose one from the sprite library, draw your own, or upload an image.

Optional official walkthrough: Sprite Creation

Your task: Add a second sprite that belongs in a data workplace.

It could be a teammate, coach, doctor, robot, customer, athlete, detective, scientist—or something completely unexpected.

STOP & DO: Add a second sprite. Click it and give that sprite its own Green Flag + say script.

8. Important discovery: sprites have their own code

If you click Sprite 1, you see Sprite 1's scripts.

If you click Sprite 2, you see Sprite 2's scripts.

This matters later when your final project has several characters doing different things.

Quick check: You added code while Sprite 2 was selected. Which sprite will run that code?

9. Give your story a place

A backdrop is the Stage's background.

Backdrops let one Scratch project feel like it has different locations or scenes.

Official Scratch Foundation: Backdrops

Add two backdrops:

  • Scene 1: a workplace or location
  • Scene 2: somewhere the problem can be investigated
STOP & DO: Add two backdrops before continuing.

10. Change scenes with code

when green flag clicked switch backdrop to [Scene 1] say “Something is wrong with our data.” for 2 seconds wait 1 seconds switch backdrop to [Scene 2]

Find switch backdrop to in Looks.

Add it to your script.

You can also use wait from Control so viewers have time to follow what is happening.

12. Break it on purpose

Programmers learn by experimenting.

Make one change and observe what happens:

  • Change move 50 to move -50.
  • Change a 2-second speech block to 5 seconds.
  • Move the scene-change block earlier.
  • Delete a block, run the project, then put it back.

Ask:

  • What did I expect?
  • What actually happened?
  • Which instruction caused the difference?
Programming idea #2: Code is something you can test, change, and test again. You do not have to understand everything before experimenting.
